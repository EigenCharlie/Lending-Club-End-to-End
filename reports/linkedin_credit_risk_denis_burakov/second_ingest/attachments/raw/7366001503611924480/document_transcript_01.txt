2. Machine Learning in the Context of IRB Models Why Trees Outperform Simpler Models? Decision trees in gradient boosted decision tree (GBDT) models provide a flexibility that linear alternatives like logistic regression lack Each tree works with the errors of the previous iterations and updates its forecasts as more trees are added to the ensemble to increase accuracy The process is somewhat similar to the Weight of Evidence (WOE) technique used by retail credit risk modellers in that we are combining evidences from different sources, in this case from different decision trees1 Gradient boosted decision trees (GBDT) 1. Machine Learning in the Context of IRB Models Understanding Gradient Boosting The process of gradient boosting can be understood as the estimation not on parameter level (as modellers do with linear and logistic regression), but in the functional space Each tree’s prediction is added to the intercept (base score) cumulatively to produce a probability or a linear prediction For regulatory use, however, these models cannot be used as is. 4. Challenges Explainability as a Regulatory Constraint? 1 3 5 The EU AI Act in conjunction with GDPR create a regulatory framework for explainability guardrails when it comes to machine learning (ML) models To comply, organizations must implement measures to make their models explainable, especially for high-risk cases (e.g., credit). This involves techniques such as feature importance and error analysis, post-hoc explanation methods to provide insights into how decisions are made By adhering to these explainability requirements, organizations not only meet regulatory standards but also enhance the overall reliability and ethical standards of their AI systems 4

Outlook for the Future Towards the Adoption of ML in the Financial Services • The adoption of Machine Learning (ML) in the area of Financial Services (FS) will continue, and FS may be the most impacted industry1 • As more Financial Services players adopt cloud technology, ML will play an ever- growing role in the provision of real-time analytical services to customers • For institutions with large retail portfolios, IRB models will likely be updated with newer algorithms, especially due to their ability to include more data sources and product segments into one model and process interactions natively • Regulatory understanding of technological trends and compliance assessments will remain key to the continuous advancements in this area in the EU ML solutions will provide more accurate risk assessments, helping institutions mitigate potential financial losses, but will require more human-in-the loop controls 1. 5 Setting the scope • ML covers a wide range of models with different level of complexity (from linear regression to deep neural network) • The discussion paper refers only to the more complex that are difficult to interpret and justify to supervisors • No clear-cut distinction between simple and complex models • The DP provides characteristics to consider to evaluate the complexity of a model Complex models High number of parameters Capacity to reflect non- linear relations Big Datasets need for a robust estimation Applicable to unstructure d data Capacity to extract info from large datasets

6 Main challenges and benefits from using ML in IRB Benefits Challenges CRR • The DP analyses CRR provisions to identify challenges and benefits in using ML to develop IRB compliant models • These are specific to the context in which ML may be used (e.g. risk differentiation or validation) and depends on whether ML does the prediction in full or it is used as a supporting tool (e.g. data cleansing) • Main concerns: 1. Interpretability of results 2. The governance with particular reference to ensuring adequate understanding by their users including management functions 3. The difficulty in evaluating the generalisation capacity of a the models

--- PAGE BREAK ---

7 Principle-based recommendations The DP stresses economic and supervisory principles that banks should consider when seeking approval to apply ML for regulatory purposes, these are aimed at:  Ensure that ML models are properly understood by their users (CRCU, validation unit and management functions)  Avoid unnecessary complexity (excessive number of parameters and reliance on unstructured data) if not justified by a significant improvement in predictive power  Ensure model can be interpreted and documented properly (assess statistical and economic relations of risk drivers with model predictions)  Enhancing the understanding of assumptions and behaviour of the model on specific predictions when using human judgement in model development and application respectively.  Justify and monitor frequent updates of a model  Focus on validation which may require increased depth/frequency
