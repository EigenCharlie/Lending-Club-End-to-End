    Collaboration Shapes Ideas and Expands Horizons One of the things I love most about this field is how ideas evolve when you invite others in. A few weeks ago, I reached out to Denis Burakov, someone I had never met in person but whose thoughtful LinkedIn posts consistently challenged and enriched my thinking. I asked him to collaborate on a paper that started as a rough draft. Denis not only said yes, he brought fresh perspectives and sharpened the ideas far beyond what I could have achieved alone. Together, we showed that Information Value (IV) and Population Stability Index (PSI), longtime staples in credit risk modeling, are not just heuristics but are grounded in information theory as Jeffreys divergence. We also: Added standard errors for IV/PSI to turn rules of thumb into formal tests. Framed performance vs. fairness as dual information objectives with uncertainty-aware constraints. Highlighted that optimal binning matters more than encoding. This work is part of a unified framework bridging decades of trusted practice with today’s fairness and responsible AI challenges, ensuring interpretability and regulatory alignment for the future. Read the paper on SSRN: https://lnkd.in/eTiTfP9w Thank you, Denis, for saying yes and for elevating this work. I’m excited for the many more collaborations ahead!

    This paper presents a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) following PRISMA guidelines, focused on credit risk prediction in unbanked and microfinance populations. The review connects methods from artificial intelligence (AI) and agent-based modeling (ABM) to identify how these approaches have been applied to financial inclusion problems. Using a semi-automated workflow built around a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline, 207 studies were screened and evaluated across six inclusion criteria, of which 95 met the established conditions. The results reveal not only clear methodological trends but also recurring weaknesses in model transparency and fairness, highlighting concrete directions for explainable and responsible credit risk modeling. https://lnkd.in/e2Aw7x-Y

    The words that "financial markets reverse course all the time," from Larry Edelman's Boston Globe Trendlines article about gold yesterday, ring true today with some indices down over 3% and gold back above 4000 (all after a tweet). I just reran an open-source market analysis prototype Shreyas Mahimkar and I built half a year ago with Tufts University's Tufts JumboHack. I used a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) on the QQQ ETF over the past two years, using only daily price change as a feature. Even without pretraining or macro inputs, the model flagged today as a hidden state shift, though to be fair, most could’ve guessed. Scrolling down to the linear regression, the market has just reverted to within one standard deviation above the line of best fit (see below). It’s a good reminder that while complex models can be fascinating, sometimes simpler ones tell the story just as well. We still don’t know what will happen next (and this is not financial advice), but Acadia Analytics is building more AI & ML tools for helping everyday traders make sense of uncertainty using data and explainable models.
