Gini is usually treated as a single summary metric, but it can actually be decomposed down to the observation level. We can compare each observation to all observations of the opposite class (defaults vs non-defaults and vice versa). The more opposite-class observations it outranks, the higher its contribution. Sum all contributions, divide by 2 (each pair is counted from both sides) → Gini. Where is this useful? ✓ Identify misranked observations hurting model performance ✓ Debug feature engineering and data quality issues ✓ Analyze outliers impacting ranking power ✓ Design policy rules for hard-to-rank cases Example: In the visualization, observations in the overlap region (scores 40-65%) contribute less to Gini.
