Historically, its origins trace back to assessing the quality of weather forecasts. Take two logistic regression models on the same credit dataset: • Model A: max PD ~31%, Gini 56% • Model B: max PD ~96%, Gini 67% Both achieve a Brier score of 8.05%. Which one do you pick? With a maximum PD of 31%, Model A lacks the range to differentiate high-risk from low-risk borrowers, making it unsuitable for building rating scales. Model B has both the range and better ranking power, but Brier won't tell you that. By decomposing the Brier score using Frank Yates' method (1982), we can see that Brier score also contains a discrimination component: 𝐶𝑜𝑣(𝑦, 𝑝) = 𝑦̄(1 − 𝑦̄) · (𝑝̄₁ − 𝑝̄₀) The discrimination reward in Brier is the base rate variance times how well the model separates average predictions between the two classes. Two models can land on the same Brier through completely different routes: one by ranking well, one by staying near the average event rate. However, Yates Decomposition (YC) math has a major flaw: its forecast variance and covariance terms can cancel each other out and hide true ranking power. We cannot look at the covariance alone to judge ranking power because its true value is contaminated by the variance. Perhaps a cleaner fix would be this relatively new paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05544. It rearranges the math into 3 simple, strictly positive factors that measure bias (Global Calibration Factor), spread (Variance Mismatch Factor), and ranking (Correlation Deficit Factor) separately. If we want to know which scorecard has the best ranking engine when BS tie, we look at exactly one factor: the Correlation Deficit. Whichever scorecard has the lower number in that specific factor wins. This score will not change just because the scorecard's variance shifts. It automatically exposes Model A's weak spread and rewards Model B's strong ranking power without needing a manual tie-breaker, i.e. trade off between variance and covariance factors in YC. …more Like Reply João Maia • 2nd Senior Data Scientist @Natura | Risco de Crédito | Desenvolvimento, Validação e Monitoramento de Modelos 1w Very insightfull Like 1 Reply Juan Pablo Usuga Cadavid • 2nd PhD | Senior Data Scientist / Machine Learning Engineer | Actuarial Science 1w Julio M.
