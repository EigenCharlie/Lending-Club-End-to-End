WOE recalibration is a middle ground: you touch one feature's likelihood ratios but leave the overall model intact. With this approach, the bin structure stays fixed, but the likelihood ratios inside them shift. Only the selected risk drivers absorb new evidence, and their WOE values are recalculated and merged back in. This matters in practice because full redevelopment is expensive and slow: new variable selection, new binning, new validation, and new approvals. Simple recalibration is fast: it adjusts the intercept without touching the features. WOE recalibration sits in between: it lets you respond to a shift in one risk driver without destabilizing the rest of the model.
