                           EDA Research on Relationship between Gini and
                           Losses for Origination Models

  ▪ The Business Case:
               ►        The Gini Coefficient is negatively correlated with the bad rate of the accepts in a given
                        population – the higher the Gini the lower the bad rate of accepts
               ►        Bad Rate of Accepts can indeed be used as a proxy for losses (assuming that Losses
                        are 100% of Exposure for Bad Accounts) so a decrease in the Bad Rate can be
                        regarded as a decrease in losses
               ►        Additionally it is expected that this effect is positively correlated to the quality of the
                        target population and the conservatism of the bank strategy – the higher the quality of
                        the target population* (lower bad rate of the population) and/or the more conservative
                        the bank (lower acceptance rate), the higher the effect of the Gini increase
  ▪ Research Assumptions:
               ►        The cumulative distribution of the goods and bads in the population follows a distribution which is a
                        variant of the normal distribution and thus can be described by its Gini-coefficient and the
                        population bad rate (a distribution function was assumed**)
               ►        The analysis is implemented for different levels of population credit quality (measured as a target
                        population bad rate) and different accept rates – the graphs in the presentation include only two
                        examples of the carried out analysis.

                           Conclusions


  ▪ For an Acceptance Rate of 60% it is expected that an Increase in GINI of 10%
    would yield a similar decrease in Accepts Bad Rate (10-11%) and therefore a
    similar decrease in losses as compared to a random model (GINI=0%)
  ▪ For a higher Acceptance Rate (80%) it is expected that a GINI increase of 10%
    would yield a lower decrease in Accepts Bad Rate and losses (about 8% for a
    Target Population* with 10% Bad Rate) as compared to a random model
    (GINI=0%)
  ▪ For a lower Acceptance Rate (40%) it is expected that a GINI increase of 10%
    would yield a higher decrease in Accepts Bad Rate and losses (about 13% for a
    Target Population* with 10% Bad Rate) as compared to a random model
    (GINI=0%)




  *Target Population is the whole population scored with the model (this is the population of applicants not hitting the policy
  reject rules)

                           Acceptance Rate effect on the Relationship between
                           Gini and %Loss Decrease

  ▪ To measure the relationship between an institution’s Acceptance Rate and the
    effect of Gini on %Losses additional research was done
  ▪ The slope of the function that expresses %Loss Decrease for a given Gini (as
    depicted on slide 3) was calculated for different Population Bad Rates
  ▪ The slope was depicted against the Acceptance Rate to illustrate the effect the
    Acceptance Rate has on %Loss Decrease as a function of the Gini
  ▪ The slope was expressed in % value which could be interpreted as how much
    would be the Loss% Decrease as a proportion of the absolute Gini increase for a
    given population bad rate and a given acceptance rate
