In the earlier chapters of this book, each had a "hero".
Chapter 1 explored Alan Turing and the Enigma, and Chapter 2 introduced Ronald Fisher and the birth of modern data science.
The hero of Chapter 3 is HAL-9000, Stanley Kubrick's fictional AI. The foundations of machine minds like HAL were laid decades earlier by Alan Turing, whose 1948 concept of "unorganised machines" anticipated the earliest neural networks.
By the mid-1950s, while AI researchers debated the nature of general intelligence at Dartmouth in 1956, the founders of FICO were already building the first scorecards, focused on interpretable, odds-based risk models.
This chapter brings these two worlds together.
It weaves the forgotten origins of neural networks, the rise of commercial credit scoring, and the later breakthroughs in backpropagation and transformers into a single narrative.
The final chapter, 𝘛𝘩𝘦 𝘊𝘳𝘦𝘥𝘪𝘵 𝘎𝘢𝘮𝘦: 𝘔𝘢𝘤𝘩𝘪𝘯𝘦 𝘓𝘦𝘢𝘳𝘯𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘌𝘹𝘱𝘭𝘢𝘪𝘯𝘢𝘣𝘪𝘭𝘪𝘵𝘺 (which will also have its own "hero"), is planned for release around Christmas 🎄.
