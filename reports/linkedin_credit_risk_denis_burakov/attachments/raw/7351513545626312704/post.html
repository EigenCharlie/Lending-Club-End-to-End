    *Build note - the pipeline behind faster, safer credit work* What’s a “data science pipeline,” practically? It’s the pathway from raw documents → decisions: collect → clean → structure → check policy → draft. Get that right and everything downstream is faster and more reliable. In our world, that looks like this (human terms): - Split long files with a bit of overlap. - Route tricky pages to OCR; read digital ones directly. - Extract just enough text to find what matters (it won’t be perfect and that’s okay). - Chunk & embed so we can search by meaning. Then ask precise questions with the original pages in view—and require receipts (citations) before we trust an answer. We’re still building and learning as we go. The goal is simple: give credit teams back the hours they lose every quarter—without black boxes. - RiskWizard #creditrisk #privatecredit #lendingops #riskmanagement #fintech

    At SAS’ recent Lending Leaders Forum, Rodney E. Hood, former Acting Comptroller of the Currency and former Chairman of the National Credit Union Administration, joined Jack Thompson, Head of US Commercial Sales at SAS, to discuss what financial institutions must prioritize as AI adoption accelerates. Rodney emphasized that AI can drive efficiency and inclusion—fraud detection, credit decisioning, customer service—but only if deployed with strong governance and fairness to avoid bias and unintended harm. He also warned that regulatory ambiguity remains one of the biggest barriers to innovation, urging banks to act now by embedding responsible practices rather than waiting for perfect clarity. This aligns with findings from the new Data and AI Impact Report: The Trust Imperative: • Banks lead in trustworthy AI efforts, yet 47% still face a trust dilemma—misalignment between confidence in AI and actual governance. • Only 11% of banks globally are in the “ideal quadrant” of high trust + strong trustworthy practices. • Organizations that invest in governance, fairness, and explainability see significantly higher ROI than those focused solely on cost-cutting. • Top challenges for banks: data governance gaps (44%), lack of specialized AI talent, and non-optimized data foundations. Rodney’s advice: Build governance frameworks now, invest in talent, and ensure AI expands access—not reinforces bias. Responsible AI isn’t just about compliance—it’s about competitiveness and trust. Words to live by here in every industry!! Read the full report for banking insights and practical steps: http://2.sas.com/6040Ah404
