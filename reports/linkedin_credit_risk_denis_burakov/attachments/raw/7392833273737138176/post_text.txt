What Alan Turing did for reasoning under uncertainty, Ronald Fisher did for learning from data. His 1922 breakthrough, the method of maximum likelihood, transformed a bewildering array of disconnected statistical techniques into a unified framework. It became the first real bridge between classical statistics and what we now call machine learning.
You'll meet the pioneers who built the foundation of every modern predictive model: Chester Bliss's probit analysis in the 1930s, Joseph Berkson's elegant logistic simplification, and Fisher's likelihood principle that now powers the softmax function at the heart of language models like Claude and ChatGPT.
This is the story of how classical statistical inference built a bridge to machine learning, with the historical details that will reveal they were never as separate as we thought.
👉Inside: hand-solved examples that build intuition, the mathematical equivalence between WOE and logistic coefficients, practical gradient descent for PD calibration, and extensions to multiclass problems with modern uncertainty quantification.
