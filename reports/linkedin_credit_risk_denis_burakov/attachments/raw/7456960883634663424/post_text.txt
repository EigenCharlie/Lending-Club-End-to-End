Integrating a new data source into a production scoring model usually means retraining from scratch. There's a better way.
Ilia Ekhlakov's recent post showed a powerful use case for credit risk data science: fine-tuning on new features as they become available, not just new records. Works for XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost models covered in 𝗑𝖻𝗈𝗈𝗌𝗍𝖾𝗋 package for ML scorecarding.
For credit risk models incorporating new data sources like credit bureaus or alternative data providers, this unlocks two things:
• Continue training with an expanded feature set
• Refresh predictions on recent data cheaply
Train a base CatBoost model, then pass it as 𝗂𝗇𝗂𝗍_𝗆𝗈𝖽𝖾𝗅 to a second fit on the wider feature set.

Original trees are frozen while new trees split on both old and new features. With SHAP-based scorecards you can trace how points redistribute: feature scores shift as the scorecard scaling adjusts to the larger set.
