When interpreting model results, people often ask: What is the recall of your model? Its F1 or precision scores? Taken in isolation from the problem, these metrics don't say much and can even mislead. Precision, recall, and F1 are often treated as universal measures of model quality but in reality they're just context-dependent tools. I came across such an example in a book by Tom Chivers. Imagine a rare event like COVID, affecting only 0.1% of people (the prevalence). Suppose you have a test that detects 90% of actual COVID cases and correctly identifies 99% of non-cases. It sure does sound great, until you look at precision: only 8% of those who tested positive actually had COVID, resulting in a low F1 score of 15%. Does that make the test useless? Not necessarily. The key is understanding what precision really is: 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗯𝗮𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗼𝗳 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗖𝗢𝗩𝗜𝗗 𝗴𝗶𝘃𝗲𝗻 𝗮 𝗽𝗼𝘀𝗶𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝘁𝗲𝘀𝘁 𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘂𝗹𝘁. This is what models like logistic regression are doing. Unlike recall, precision depends on the overall prevalence of the condition, so even highly accurate models can produce low precision and F1 scores when the event you're looking for is rare.     #100daysofML Day 41 — Bias–Variance Trade-off The Bias–Variance Trade-off explains the balance between underfitting and overfitting in a model. High Bias: Model is too simple, leading to underfitting. High Variance: Model is too complex, capturing noise and overfitting. A good ML model aims to minimize both bias and variance to achieve optimal generalization. 💡 Example: A linear model on a curved dataset has high bias (underfitting), while a 10th-degree polynomial has high variance (overfitting). ✅ Advantages: Helps understand model performance balance. Guides tuning model complexity. ⚠️ Disadvantages: Hard to quantify bias and variance separately.

    #100daysofML Day 15: Feature Engineering & Feature Selection Feature Engineering is the process of creating new features from existing data to improve model performance, while Feature Selection means choosing the most relevant features and removing unnecessary ones. Good features make ML models smarter and reduce complexity. This step includes scaling, encoding, polynomial features, feature splitting, and dimensionality reduction. Example: Feature Engineering: Creating BMI = weight / height² from height & weight. Feature Selection: Removing irrelevant features like "Passenger ID" in Titanic dataset. Advantages: Improves model accuracy and interpretability. Reduces overfitting by removing irrelevant data. Decreases training time. Disadvantages: Requires strong domain knowledge. Over-engineering can add noise instead of value. Wrong feature selection may drop useful data.
