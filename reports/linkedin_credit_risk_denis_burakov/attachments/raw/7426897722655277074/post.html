Loss Given Default (LGD) is a key risk parameter that determines credit loss. It is central to regulatory capital, loan pricing, and portfolio risk management. However, there is no single "right" way to model LGD, so exploring alternatives is always worth the effort. In a short paper I explore 𝘱𝘳𝘰𝘣𝘢𝘣𝘪𝘭𝘪𝘴𝘵𝘪𝘤 𝘳𝘦𝘨𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘴𝘪𝘰𝘯 𝘷𝘪𝘢 𝘲𝘶𝘢𝘯𝘵𝘪𝘭𝘦-𝘣𝘢𝘴𝘦𝘥 𝘤𝘭𝘢𝘴𝘴𝘪𝘧𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘵𝘪𝘰𝘯: instead of predicting 𝘌[𝘓𝘎𝘋|𝘟] directly, we bin LGD into quantiles, fit a multiclass classifier for 𝘗(𝘣𝘪𝘯 𝘬 | 𝘟), and take the point estimate as the probability-weighted sum of bin midpoints. This gives a full conditional distribution in one model. This approach captures LGD without normality assumptions, produces calibrated predictions, and facilitates stress testing, regulatory capital, and IFRS 9 applications. Future work will explore probabilistic LGD intervals and RWA impact using the proposed methodology. #CreditRiskModeling #DataScience #Lending #IRB #IFRS9

    Loss Given Default (LGD): The Most Underrated Risk Metric In most credit risk conversations, Probability of Default (PD) steals the spotlight. But the real question every risk professional should be asking is: - If the borrower defaults… how much will we actually lose? That’s where Loss Given Default (LGD) becomes the real game-changer. 💡 What LGD Really Means LGD is the percentage of exposure we don’t recover after a default. It’s not just about if they fail—it’s about what’s left when they do. LGD is driven by: - Quality of collateral (not just its presence) - Loan-to-Value (LTV) at time of default - Recovery timelines and legal jurisdiction - Enforcement strength - Broader economic conditions Two borrowers can default on the same day—but the losses can tell two completely different stories. ⚠️ Why LGD Is Harder to Get Right Than PD PD asks: Who might default? LGD asks: What happens next? And that’s where things get messy: - Market uncertainty at time of default 📉 - Volatility in asset prices 🏠 - Legal delays and restructuring timelines ⏳ - Recovery and servicing costs 💸 In downturns, defaults rise—but losses explode when LGD spikes. 📘 The IFRS 9 & Governance Angle Under IFRS 9, LGD can’t just be a historical average. It must be: - Forward-looking - Built on reasonable and supportable assumptions - Grounded in actual recovery outcomes Relying too much on the past can hide the true cost of future stress. 🧠 Final Thought PD tells you who might fall. LGD tells you how much it will hurt when they do. Strong credit risk management isn’t just about spotting trouble early—it’s about being realistic about the damage when trouble arrives. #CreditRisk #RiskManagement #LGD #IFRS9 #CreditAnalysis

    Streamlining Credit Risk Capital Requirements: The EBA’s latest push for a simplified framework The European Banking Authority (EBA) recently published a significant discussion paper aimed at simplifying the credit risk framework. It’s a welcome move towards reducing complexity and enhancing efficiency, without compromising the risk sensitivity we’ve worked hard to establish. This paper marks a clear direction for the industry, and there are three key areas that deserve our attention: 1. Refinement of the Standardised Approach (SA) for Real Estate The focus is on achieving greater harmonisation through clearer distinctions. By precisely defining residential versus commercial properties and sharpening the criteria for Income-Producing Real Estate (IPRE), the EBA is positioning the Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio as the primary driver of risk sensitivity. 2. A more cohesive IRB Approach Complexity in Internal Ratings-Based models has long been a challenge. The proposal suggests harmonising testing requirements and providing much-needed clarity on definitions—such as what constitutes a 'facility'—ensuring more consistent application across all risk parameters. 3. Pragmatic estimation methods To support practical implementation, the EBA is introducing optional fallback methods for estimating LGD and CCF, as well as more streamlined approaches for the 'Margin of Conservatism' (MoC). These proposals reflect a pragmatic shift towards a more navigable regulatory landscape. We will be providing a detailed analysis of these developments on our PwC Regulatory Blog shortly.     The EBA published a Discussion Paper this month looking at whether the credit risk framework built after 2008 has become more complex than the risks it was designed to manage. EBA/DP/2026/01 | Consultation open until 10 May 2026 ── STANDARDISED APPROACH – REAL ESTATE CRR Article 430a requires loss data, yet "loss" has no agreed definition. SA and IRB institutions calculate it differently – making cross-institution comparisons unreliable for supervisory decisions. The EBA is also asking whether the mix of national exceptions on real estate risk weights still forms a single consistent framework. IRB APPROACH – SIMPLIFICATION OPTIONS Guidelines on PD/LGD estimation, Downturn LGD, and CCF estimation (currently in consultation) were written at different times, sit in separate documents, and overlap. The plan is to merge them without changing what they cover. Simpler fallback options are being explored where modelling effort now outweighs what it captures – across Margin of Conservatism, Downturn LGD, direct and indirect costs, and CCF estimation. The 12-month fixed horizon requirement for CCF creates bias at every stage; the EBA is proposing greater flexibility toward a cohort approach. To judge whether any Level 1 change is worth making, EBA proposes six criteria: → Criticality of exposures → Materiality of miscalibration → Simplicity of rules → Transition costs → Intrinsic consistency → Basel alignment ── The BCBS's December 2022 evaluation of Basel III did not answer whether the same resilience could have been achieved with a less complex framework – stating there is no other scenario to compare with. The complexity under review was built deliberately to narrow RWA variability and restore trust in internal models. The harder question this paper raises is not whether simplification is useful – it is which parts of the current complexity were doing actual prudential work, and which were doing administrative work dressed up as prudential. 📄 Read the full Discussion Paper: https://lnkd.in/e-8R2YwS #CreditRisk #EBA #BankingRegulation #IRBModels #BaselIII
