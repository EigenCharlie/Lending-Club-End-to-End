🧭 In 1941, Alan M. Turing turned wartime guesswork into arithmetic. He took odds in favor of a hypothesis (matching texts), logged them, and built an additive scoring system, a framework that later became foundational in credit scoring. This chapter traces that lineage from Turing's Factor Principle at Bletchley Park, through Jack Good and David MacKay, to modern credit scoring and explainable AI.
