Visualizing Gradient Boosting 🌳
You've probably come across various explanations of how gradient boosting works. In a nutshell, gradient boosting is an ensemble technique where each new decision tree learns from the mistakes (pseudo-residuals) of the previous trees, iteratively improving the model’s predictions. Yet, despite this clear explanation, the process can still feel abstract 🌀
To bring this concept to life, I've created a visualization that demonstrates the dynamics of gradient boosting in action 🔎 ⤵
• Positive cases are marked with a ➕ symbol.
• Negative cases are marked with a ➖ symbol.
• The size of each symbol represents the residuals, i.e. how far off the model's prediction was from the true label.
How it works 🔄
• Initially, the model may misclassify several examples, leading to large residuals (and thus larger symbols).
• As the boosting process continues, the model adjusts predictions by adding new trees that correct these mistakes.
• Over successive iterations, incorrectly classified examples with large residuals are gradually corrected, reducing their size and improving accuracy.
In this example, I'm using additive logistic regression (LogitBoost), a variant of gradient boosting closely related to the classic logistic regression, to show how each iteration refines the model to boost accuracy 📈
Watch the animation below to see how gradient boosting transforms residuals into powerful predictions, one tree at a time! 🌟
