           Binary and Multiclass Modeling
A lot of applied problems are modeled as a binary choice (e.g., credit decisions),
which is a compact way to decompose observed real-world phenomena.
Multiclass models take this one step further by expanding the range of
outcomes (e.g., the decision to approve a credit line, credit card, or a BNPL)




Binary and multiclass approaches are fundamental to modeling a wide variety
of real-world scenarios because they allow us to both score and categorize
outcomes, which is useful in numerous business applications

In this deck, we focus on logistic regression and gradient boosted decision tree
(GBDT) models to showcase these two modeling paradigms in action ✨

           Binary and Multiclass Modeling
Most texts skip the important connection between binary and multiclass
models for problems with two outcomes, often jumping directly to a K > 2 case

We can draw down these two approaches schematically, the first predicting
only class 1 (binary) and the second predicting classes 0 and 1 (multiclass) ⤵




Even though the link functions to produce class probabilities are different
between the models (logistic vs softmax), they converge to the same linear
decision boundary at their maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) in this case

In what follows next we introduce the equivalence of the two methods in binary
problem scenarios like predicting credit default 🏦 ➡

The issue with linear models for classification is that due to the linear nature
of weights (coefficients), the model loses a lot of flexibility in modeling classes

We can clearly see this if we compare performance of logistic regression with
the multinomial logistic regression on the same underlying data ⤵




Notice the drop in rank-ordering performance (Gini) for multinomial logistic
regression model vs logistic regression due to the inclusion of more classes

The prob_1_and_2 is the complement of prob_0 (class 0 prediction), because due

           Binary and Multiclass Modeling
When using linear models, turning a binary problem into a multiclass one
reduces performance for the defaulted vs non-defaulted class. However, more
flexible ML algorithms, like gradient boosted decision trees (GBDT), can
leverage class information to improve binary ranking performance

We repeat the same experiment with XGBoost and CatBoost using the same set
of hyperparameters for comparison, i.e. 100 decision trees with a depth of 2




It turns out that using a multiclass GBDT model can actually give a noticeable
improvement in terms of the discriminatory power over default vs non-default
risk differentiation
