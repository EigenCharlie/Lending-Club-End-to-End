1    Introduction
Loss Given Default (LGD) is a foundational concept in credit risk modeling. LGD measures
the credit loss a bank is likely to incur due to an obligor default [2], and is widely used for
regulatory capital calculation, pricing, and management reporting.

In statistical modeling, LGD is defined as follows:

2       LGD Modeling
In this section we cover conventional approaches to statistical modeling of LGD1 and offer an
alternative methodology focused on modern machine learning applications.


2.1     Conventional Approaches

The most common approach to modeling LGD is a linear regression model. It relies on the
assumption of normality of errors, which is typically violated. However, due to its simplicity
and interpretability of results, it is often the go-to method in many situations.

Alternative methodologies include modeling LGD in two steps:

 • Estimating the probability of loss: P (LGD > 0)

 • Modeling the expected loss given default: E[LGD | LGD > 0, X]
    1
    Note that depending on model design and modularity, LGD estimates can further incorporate cure events,
debt sales, and other outcomes within a single modeling framework, though such extensions are beyond the
scope of this paper.

The resulting LGD estimate is then obtained by combining these components:


                  E[LGD | X] = P (LGD > 0 | X) · E[LGD | LGD > 0, X]                          (2)

While this approach better captures the bimodal nature of LGD and accounts for zero-loss
events, it introduces additional complexity: two separate models must be estimated and
validated, each requiring its own set of assumptions and diagnostics.


2.2     Probabilistic Regression via Quantile-Based Classification

Standard regression assumes constant variance. However, LGD exhibits distributional
complexities:

 • Some borrowers display low-or-high LGD patterns, leading to bimodality

 • Different tail behaviors by segment result in skewness

To account for this, we can reformulate the problem as multiclass classification over discretized
LGD bins, following the approach outlined in [3].

2.2.1   Methodology

Rather than predicting a point estimate ŷ = E[LGD | X], this method predicts the full
conditional distribution P (LGD ∈ bink | X) for k = 1, . . . , K bins:

 • Discretize the LGD variable into K bins using quantile-based binning

 • Train a multiclass classifier to predict P (LGD ∈ bink | X)

 • Obtain probability distribution of LGD: p(X) = [p1 (X), p2 (X), . . . , pK (X)]

 • Compute LGD point estimate: ŷ =          k=1 pk (X) · vk
                                           PK


Here vk is the midpoint of the k-th bin. Unlike standard regression with homoscedastic
assumptions, this approach naturally models the characteristic bimodal LGD distribution
with mass at 0 and 1.

2.2.4   Experiments

In our experiments, we use the LGD dataset provided in [1] to model LGD as a function of
Loan-to-Value (LTV) and loan purpose. The dataset is split into 70% training and 30% test
sets. For the quantile multiclass model, we use 10 quantile bins for the LGD target.

We compare the proposed method against a baseline CatBoost regressor on the original LGD
and log-transformed LGD distribution:

 • Quantile-based (midpoint): Bin representatives are the midpoints of bin edges

 • Baseline regressor: Standard CatBoost regression with squared loss

3     Conclusion
This paper presented a quantile-based multiclass classification approach for LGD modeling
that addresses the inherent bimodality and skewness of LGD distributions.

Our experimental results demonstrate that this framework achieves competitive predictive per-
formance compared to standard regression while providing substantially superior calibration
and native support for probability distributions.

The key advantage of this approach lies in its ability to serve multiple regulatory and business
requirements from a single model: computing downturn LGD percentiles for capital adequacy,
performing stress testing through distribution shifting, and enabling scenario-based expected
credit loss calculations under IFRS 9.

By reformulating LGD estimation as classification over quantile bins, the method provides the
interpretability and efficiency of traditional approaches while addressing their fundamental
limitation, the inability to capture distributional uncertainty.

Future work will explore LGD prediction intervals and Risk Weighted Assets (RWA) simula-
tions using the proposed methodology.
