Next Patch Prediction for
AutoRegressive Visual Generation

Abstract

Autoregressive models, built based on the Next Token Prediction (NTP) paradigm, show great potential in developing a unified framework that integrates both language and vision tasks. In this work, we rethink the NTP for autoregressive image generation and propose a novel Next Patch Prediction (NPP) paradigm. Our key idea is to group and aggregate image tokens into patch tokens containing high information density. With patch tokens as a shorter input sequence, the autoregressive model is trained to predict the next patch, thereby significantly reducing the computational cost. We further propose a multi-scale coarse-to-fine patch grouping strategy that exploits the natural hierarchical property of image data. Experiments on a diverse range of models (100M-1.4B parameters) demonstrate that the next patch prediction paradigm could reduce the training cost to ∼0.6×\sim 0.6\times∼ 0.6 × while improving image generation quality by up to 1.0 FID score on the ImageNet benchmark. We highlight that our method retains the original autoregressive model architecture without introducing additional trainable parameters or specifically designing a custom image tokenizer, thus ensuring flexibility and seamless adaptation to various autoregressive models for visual generation.

1 Introduction

Autoregressive models, foundational to large language models (LLMs) (Vaswani et al., 2017; Devlin et al., 2018; Radford et al., 2018; Raffel et al., 2020; Radford et al., 2019; Brown et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2022), generate content through the prediction of subsequent tokens in a sequence. This Next Token Prediction paradigm enables LLMs to excel in a variety of natural language processing tasks, exhibiting human-like conversational abilities (Ouyang et al., 2022; OpenAI, 2022; 2023; Google, 2023; Anthropic, 2023; Workshop et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023a; b; Bai et al., 2023a; Yang et al., 2023; Team, 2023; Bi et al., 2024) and demonstrating remarkable scalability (Kaplan et al., 2020; Henighan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2022; Alabdulmohsin et al., 2022; Chowdhery et al., 2023; Anil et al., 2023). Such advancements illustrate the potential for achieving general-purpose artificial intelligence systems.

Inspired by the success of autoregressive models in the language domain, their applications for image generation are significantly explored. Notable approaches, including VQVAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017; Razavi et al., 2019), VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2022), DALL-E (Ramesh et al., 2021), and Parti (Yu et al., 2021; 2022), introduce image tokenizers that convert continuous images into discrete tokens, employing autoregressive models to sequentially generate these tokens, thereby achieving image generation. In parallel, diffusion models (Song & Ermon, 2019; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Lu et al., 2022a; Ho et al., 2022a; Ho & Salimans, 2022; Rombach et al., 2022; Ramesh et al., 2022; Saharia et al., 2022; Rombach et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023; Podell et al., 2023; Xue et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a; b; Betker et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024a; Esser et al., 2024) emerge as a distinct and rapidly evolving paradigm in image generation. With wide open-source community support, diffusion models dominate the visual generation tasks. However, the fundamental differences in the underlying methodologies of autoregressive and diffusion models pose significant challenges for developing a unified framework that integrates both language and vision tasks. More recently, a pioneering work LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024) achieves the next token prediction paradigm for image generation with a vanilla autoregressive model, Llama, bringing the field one step closer to building a unified model between language and vision.

In this work, we follow LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024) for autoregressive image generation and rethink the modeling of the NTP paradigm in the following aspects.

a). The NTP paradigm, widely successful in large language models, leverages the high information density of text tokens. However, this property does not directly transfer to image data, where information density is naturally low compared to language data. Our key insight is to process image tokens into high information density units (i.e., patches), potentially leading to better performance in autoregressive image generation.

b). Transformer-based autoregressive models incur significant computational costs during training, with the total cost approximately scaling as C≈6⁢W⁢N𝐶6𝑊𝑁C\approx 6WNitalic_C ≈ 6 italic_W italic_N, where W𝑊Witalic_W represents the number of model parameters and N𝑁Nitalic_N denotes the input sequence length. While maintaining the model architecture, we could manage to reduce the input length of image tokens, improving training efficiency.

c). Unlike language data, the image modality inherently exhibits a hierarchical property in understanding and generation. This suggests that the training process for autoregressive image generation could be modeled in a multi-scale, coarse-to-fine manner, potentially enhancing generation quality.

Building on these insights, we introduce Next Patch Prediction (NPP), a simple yet effective method for autoregressive visual generation. Specifically, the input image tokens are grouped and aggregated into patch tokens of higher information density through an intra-patch average operation. With the resulting patch tokens as a shorter input sequence, the autoregressive model is trained to predict the next patch, thus significantly reducing the computational cost. We further propose a multi-scale patch grouping strategy, modeling patches in a coarse-to-fine manner, which enhances training efficiency and generation quality. As shown in Figure 1, experiments on a diverse range of models from 100M to 1.4B parameters demonstrate that the next patch prediction paradigm could reduce the training cost to ∼0.6×\sim 0.6\times∼ 0.6 × while improving image generation quality by up to 1.0 FID score on the ImageNet benchmark. We highlight that our method retains the original autoregressive model architecture without introducing additional trainable parameters or specifically designing a custom image tokenizer, ensuring flexibility for seamless adaptation to various autoregressive models addressing visual generation tasks.

3.1 Setup

Benchmark. We build the Next Patch Prediction based on LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024) and evaluate it on the class-conditional image generation task using the standard ImageNet1K benchmark (Deng et al., 2009).

Model Architecture. For the image encoder, we adopt the same VQGAN tokenizer trained by LlamaGen on ImageNet1K. The tokenizer has a vocabulary size of 16,384 and downsamples the input image at a fixed ratio of 16×16161616\times 1616 × 16. For the autoregressive model, we adopt the same setting as LlamaGen. Note that our method does NOT introduce any extra trainable parameters and thereby can be easily extended to other autoregressive models or scaling up to similar tasks.

4 Related Works

4.1 Visual Generation

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) (Goodfellow et al., 2014; Brock et al., 2018; Karras et al., 2019; Kang et al., 2023) are the pioneering method for visual generation in the deep learning era, focusing on learning to generate realistic images through adversarial training. Inspired by language model architectures, BERT-style models (Chang et al., 2022; 2023; Yu et al., 2023a; b) emerge, using masked-prediction techniques to learn to predict missing parts of images, much like how BERT predicts masked words in text. Autoregressive models (Esser et al., 2021; Ramesh et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2022), inspired by GPT, predict the next token in a sequence. These methods often involve an image tokenization step (Kingma & Welling, 2013; Van Den Oord et al., 2017), converting pixel space into a more semantically meaningful representation. Diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song & Ermon, 2019; Song et al., 2020; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Rombach et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2024a) introduce a novel approach, treating visual generation as a reverse diffusion process, where images are gradually denoised from Gaussian noise through a series of steps.

4.2 Multimodal Foundation Models

Recent advancements in vision-and-language models (Liu et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2024; Peng et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Pagnoni et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024a; Lin et al., 2023; 2024b; Jin et al., 2024) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in visual understanding through visual instruction tuning. However, unifying the understanding and generation tasks in multimodal models is still being explored. Most existing approaches (Sun et al., 2023b; a; Dong et al., 2024; Ge et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b; Gu et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024b; Tong et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024c; Deng et al., 2024) focus on integrating diffusion models with other existing pre-trained models, rather than adopting a unified next-token prediction paradigm. These methods often require complex designs to link two distinct training paradigms, which makes scaling up more challenging. Some works (Yu et al., 2024b; Qu et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024; Han et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024) focus on image tokenizer for better compression and reconstruction of image data, which is crucial for the image generation quality. Some pioneering efforts (Lu et al., 2022b; 2023; Bai et al., 2023b; Team et al., 2023; Team, 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2024; Pang et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024a; He et al., 2024) explore incorporating image generation into large language models using an autoregressive approach, achieving promising results. However, most of them do not focus on the autoregressive image generation method or directly adopt the next token prediction paradigm. More recently, some efforts Shao et al. (2024); Pagnoni et al. (2024); The et al. (2024) for LLM study novel sequence modeling methods, proposing to aggregate text tokens into units (i.e., patch or concept) with higher information density. In this work, we draw inspiration from them and propose a novel next patch prediction paradigm for autoregressive visual generation without introducing additional trainable parameters or specifically designing a custom image tokenizer, ensuring flexibility for seamless adaptation to various autoregressive image generation tasks.

5 Limitation and Future Works

Currently, due to limited computational resources, we are unable to provide experimental results on text-to-image tasks or develop a unified vision-language model. However, our method retains the original autoregressive model architecture without introducing additional trainable parameters or specifically designing a custom image tokenizer. We believe it can be seamlessly adapted to various autoregressive models addressing similar tasks, offering a baseline framework for future exploration. For future works, exploring the Next Patch Prediction paradigm for autoregressive video generation is an interesting direction, where the input sequence length is significantly longer, resulting in higher computational costs. By leveraging our method, we anticipate improved efficiency and scalability in handling the extended sequences inherent in video data. Another potential research direction is to explore more effective methods for obtaining patch embeddings. In this work, we adopt a simple intra-patch averaging operation, which is straightforward but may be limited in its capacity to capture fine-grained details. Designing more powerful and expressive patch extraction methods could further improve training efficiency and enhance generation quality.

6 Conclusion

In this work, we introduce a novel Next Patch Prediction paradigm that improves autoregressive image generation by grouping and aggregating image tokens into high-density patch tokens. This approach reduces the computational cost to ∼0.6×\sim 0.6\times∼ 0.6 × while improving image generation quality by up to 1.0 FID score on the ImageNet benchmark. Our method retains the original autoregressive model architecture without introducing additional trainable parameters or custom image tokenizers, thereby making the next patch prediction paradigm seamlessly adapted to various autoregressive models addressing image generation tasks.
