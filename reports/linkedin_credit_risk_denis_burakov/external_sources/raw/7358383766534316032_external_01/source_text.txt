We are thrilled to announce that Mackenzie Weygandt Mathis, Bertarelli Foundation Chair of Integrative Neuroscience at EPFL and co-creator of DeepLabCut (the open-source deep learning framework that has redefined markerless pose estimation across neuroscience, medicine, and biology) will deliver a keynote at Compute! Paris 2026!
A leading voice in open science and adaptive intelligence, Mackenzie has built tools - from DeepLabCut to CEBRA - that are now used by thousands of labs worldwide to decode behavior and neural activity. Her work has been recognized with the Eric Kandel Young Neuroscientist Prize (2023), the Swiss Science Prize Latsis (2024) and the SGV Award (2025).
At the heart of her work lies a fundamental question: how do brains adapt to an ever-changing world? Her lab studies the mechanisms of adaptive sensorimotor behavior in intelligent systems - “adaptive intelligence” - while building the open-source machine learning tools, from DeepLabCut to CEBRA, that make this science possible.
Don’t miss this opportunity to engage with the most influential voices in scientific computing and open-source innovation, alongside the builders of tools like Jupyter, Scikit-learn, and Apache Arrow.
📆 Dates: November 25–26, 2026
📍 Venue: Centre International de Conférences de Sorbonne Université, Paris
🚨 The CfP is open until May 24th!
Compute! is a new annual conference for those working with open-source computation and data, designed for people who want to understand, not just use.
