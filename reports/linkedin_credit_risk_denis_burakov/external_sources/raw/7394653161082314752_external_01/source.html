About us

At LocalStack, we provide the #1 platform for local cloud development & testing, offering the most advanced cloud emulation for AWS services. Our aim is to assist individual cloud developers and enterprises in becoming solution nimble, optimizing costs, reducing contention, and delivering resilient and secure value to their customers. LocalStack is ideal for designing and testing highly resilient and secure solutions on your local machine before live deployment. Our platform facilitates integration testing of cloud solutions both locally and in CI/CD environments, enhancing developer velocity and feature quality. With LocalStack, you fully emphasize on black-box solution tests that fully exercise the inputs and outputs of your system, rather than solely focusing on unit tests. Our solution also enables rapid design and development of sophisticated cloud solutions with little to no changes needed for IaC pipelines. In addition, by emulating cloud services locally, LocalStack enables additional features and workflows that are not feasible on the cloud, such as local chaos engineering, IAM security testing, team collaboration, and more!

LocalStack

DevOps Software

LocalStack is a cloud development platform that facilitates building/testing of cloud and serverless applications on your local machine. At its core, it features a service emulator that runs in a single container on your laptop or in your CI environment. LocalStack supports a growing number of AWS services, like Lambda, S3, DynamoDB, SQS, RDS, ECS, EKS and many more! With LocalStack, you can run your AWS applications or Lambdas entirely on your local machine without connecting to a remote cloud provider! Whether you are testing complex CDK applications or Terraform configurations, or just beginning to learn about AWS services, LocalStack helps speed up and simplify your testing and development workflow.

    You've typed it a thousand times: kubectl apply. One command, then your pod just… shows up somewhere. But what actually happens in between? Kiah Imani just published a fantastic breakdown of the full 7-step Kubernetes deployment lifecycle...every component, every handoff, and how to watch it happen live using LocalStack. Here's the map: → Step 1: kubectl serializes your YAML and POSTs to the API Server → Step 2: Auth, authorization, and schema validation (all three must pass before etcd is written) → Step 3: The Controller Manager sees the new Deployment and creates a ReplicaSet → Step 4: The ReplicaSet controller writes Pod objects (intentions, not running containers yet) → Step 5: The Scheduler scores nodes and assigns a destination (it doesn't start anything) → Step 6: kubelet pulls the image, mounts volumes, and starts the container → Step 7: Readiness probes pass, EndpointSlices update, iptables rules propagate (now traffic can reach it) The mental model is most useful when things break. Pod stuck in Pending? That's Step 5 — the Scheduler can't find a valid node. ImagePullBackOff? Step 6. Readiness probe failing? Step 7. Once you know which step failed, you know which component to look at. The post also walks through spinning up a local EKS cluster with LocalStack, deploying a real workload, and deliberately killing a pod to watch the reconciliation loop self-heal in real time. Worth a read whether you're deep into platform engineering or just want to finally understand what Kubernetes is actually doing. 🔗 https://lnkd.in/eycZmWmU #Kubernetes #EKS #PlatformEngineering #CloudNative #LocalStack #DevOps #SRE

    Solving the "Static Environment" Bottleneck 😅 In a traditional cloud-native SDLC, pre-production environments are often "static." They persist over time, accumulating configuration drift and state pollution that makes debugging non-deterministic. LocalStack Cloud Pods allow architects to treat infrastructure state as version-controlled artifacts, enabling a "Snapshot-and-Share" workflow for cloud resources. Use LocalStack Cloud Pods for... 🏗️ Architectural Advantages State Serialization to capture the full runtime state of your local AWS stack (including DynamoDB records, S3 objects, and IAM configurations) into a portable "Pod." 🐞 Collaborative Debugging Instead of sending logs or screenshots, senior engineers can share a Cloud Pod. A peer can pod load the exact state to reproduce a bug in seconds, ensuring 100% environment parity. 🌱 Deterministic CI Seeding Use Cloud Pods to pre-seed CI pipelines with complex datasets or specific infrastructure states, reducing the time spent on "Setup" scripts and ensuring every test run starts from a known-good baseline. 🚦 Rapid Context Switching High-velocity teams can switch between feature branches by loading different Pods, effectively swapping entire cloud architectures without manual reconfiguration. Move away from shared, persistent staging environments and drastically improve the fidelity of your integration testing. https://lnkd.in/eMYSdNJi
