    TWO WEEKS WITH TWO NVIDIA DGX SPARKS - 8 oss models benched - 3 code harnesses integrated - 1 clustered model across both sparks - 1 unattended coding run for 7+ hours So, it's been two weeks testing out the DGX Dparks. Am I tired of having fun? Now is my last opportunity to return these golden NUCs for a refund. Should I return them and invest in a monster GPU or a stacked Mac Studio, instead? Actually, no. I don't want to. These machines are keepers. They feel like the Nintendo NES, original and one of a kind. Sure, they are not as fast or wide as the frontier. And they decode slower than Apple Silicon. But they also prefill much faster. It's true that the quality of open source models are really not that far behind the frontier today - and now I have experienced that fact personally. I've had two full days now with two models plugged into PI Dev that make local llm assisted development feel golden: 1. DeepSeek v4 Flash Q2 Dwarf Star - Planning and & Orchestration x1 2. Qwen 3.6 30B MoE Thinking - Coding & Execution x16 The whole experience feels very close to Claude Code. I've had time to set up PI Dev with AGENTS .md, pulled a few skills from Claude Code, and created a custom PI extension to use the Sparks as an API endpoint. I already love it more than Claude Code, or Cursor, where I always seemed to be fighting with something they recently broke. Playing with agentics on the Sparks is a special kind of fun. The Sparks have inspired me to code without AI. 😂. I'm currently crafting a set of agentic primitives as a base for building out new harnesses quickly. This is the kind of code that I want to reuse over and over in different products - code that deserves that old fashioned line-by-line human attention. I want to test out some multimedia generation models over the coming weeks, and build out local utilities to swap configurations quickly for whatever kind of work I'm doing. So, the Sparks stay. #ai #llms #agents #spark #harness #sparksetup

    I'm holding one of the most anticipated Canadian fintech products of 2026. Wealthsimple's no FX fee credit card. Here's how we got here: American Express Platinum used to cover foreign transaction fees. Then they removed it; no big announcement, just gone. That left Scotiabank Passport as basically the only real option. A card that makes you park $6,000 with them just to waive the annual fee. One option. Take it or leave it. Home Trust had a no-FX card too, but almost nobody knew it existed. So when Wealthsimple announced this card, people joined a waitlist before the product was even officially released. That's not hype. That's what happens when a market has been underserved for years. Canadians are quietly paying 2.5% on every foreign purchase. On a $5K trip, that's $125 gone for nothing. The irony? I'm writing this from an airport lounge, card in hand and yet I'm still carrying cash because getting money out of a foreign ATM without fees is somehow still unsolved. We've figured out investing. Spending abroad is stuck in 2005. Fix the ATM problem next, Wealthsimple. 👀
