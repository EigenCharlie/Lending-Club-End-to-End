Weight-of-Evidence (WOE) in Credit Scoring 🎲
WOE is a beloved technique of Credit Risk Modellers. It’s simple, reliable, and very effective on structured underwriting datasets. Contrary to what many people believe is a simple logistic regression, WOE can be used in more complex ensemble models like Bagging or Boosting showing performance similar to out-of-the-box XGBoost while remaining similar to a regulatory white-box 🎐

Thanks for sharing the post and papers. WoE encoding, along with the binning of numeric risk factors, is probably the most common approach in standard credit risk modeling today. I've noticed also a common belief among practitioners regarding WoE's superiority over other encoding methods. I find this questionable as generalization in practice depends on various factors, such as characteristics of the modeling datasets, alternative encodings, performance metrics, and the binning process.
A main weakness practitioners point out with WoE (and other target-based encoders) is the potential for model overfitting, given the use of target values to transform variables.
In my opinion, when developing the model, especially within the traditional modeling framework, it's worth testing different encoding methods and making the final decision based on a combination of model performance, business inputs, and operational ease.
By the way, a type of encoding not that common in practice but which I find very convenient is nested dummy encoding (aka partitions). It effectively addresses the multivariate binning process combined with the business requirement of different risk profiles between adjacent bins.

Denis Burakov Thanks for sharing, interesting read. Performance of other model candidates in this example is very close to WoE baseline - what is the added benefit of XGBoost usage here ? Results displayed add weight to the evidence (pun intended) that WoE + Logistic regression is a relatively simple yet powerful combination, which has stood the test of time, atleast in the area of Credit Risk model development. XGBoost and RandomForest can be used for feature building and selection, and for uncovering more complex relationships in the data. Also, as Jakob Lavröd commented performing WoE discretization leads to a loss of information as the modeller imposes a particular relationship between the bin values and bad/default rates. Constraining the information set before using ensemble approaches like RandomForest and XGBoost can be thought of as handicap, as there could be other, useful relationships in the data, apart from those imposed during WoE analysis.
