𝐈𝐅𝐑𝐒𝟗 𝐅𝐨𝐫𝐰𝐚𝐫𝐝-𝐋𝐨𝐨𝐤𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐞𝐥𝐢𝐧𝐠 For me, forward-looking (FLI) modeling under IFRS9 is one of the most interesting areas of credit risk modeling because it goes far beyond a purely statistical exercise. To complete it successfully, practitioners must understand not only the behavior and development of the modeled target but also the key historical events that shaped it - and anticipate potential future events that could influence it as well. Practitioners often face serious challenges with this exercise, arguing that a limited number of observations makes it impossible to capture all relevant business and statistical aspects. But is that always the case? Are there methods that can help overcome this limitation and provide a good balance between necessary inputs while keeping bias under control? Over the past year, FLI modeling has been one of the topics I have explored most extensively, resulting in numerous presentations and experiments. For those interested, below is a list of links covering specific aspects of FLI modeling. It is crucial to carefully align the model’s complexity, understandability, and interpretability with the portfolio’s size & complexity, as well as with the economic, financial, risk management, mathematical, and statistical expertise of the managers who use the models to manage the portfolio. Many modelers assume that building the most accurate model is enough, which often makes it less interpretable for those with a-bit-above-average knowledge in the aforementioned areas; in daily operations, this does not improve portfolio management but often leads to a ‘black-box’ effect (unnoticed by modelers who fully understand their creations). I’ve seen this repeatedly in my banking and consulting career, in small & medium local banks, and in groups where models are developed centrally and rolled out to subsidiaries The result? Local managers treat the model like magic; they don’t question it, don’t adjust it properly, and don’t learn from it
