When I created my GitHub repository Applied Data Science for Credit Risk (https://lnkd.in/dm2p5UNv), I intended it to be a personal space where I could summarize some of my work related to various credit risk modeling topics. However, over the last two years, it has grown rapidly and now contains hundreds of pages of presentations and working papers, thousands of lines of R and Python code, and has also attracted the interest of other practitioners in the field. It now serves as a supporting repository for my books, with the main goal of extending their scope and addressing some of the challenges practitioners face in their everyday work in the IRB and IFRS9 areas. Regular writing and publishing have helped me think more deeply about certain aspects of common practices and have provided a consistent source of new ideas and motivation for further research.
For those who find it interesting and useful, below is a list of materials published since the beginning of this year.
In the coming months, I plan to post more on similar topics, including new ideas for R and Python packages, as well as discussions of topics that challenge current common practices in this area. I hope the material will remain as interesting to others as it is to me.
