Backtesting PD models is typically one of the most comprehensive validation exercises performed during model development and validation.
In practice, three statistical tests are commonly employed for backtesting PD estimates at the level of individual years: the exact binomial test, the z-score test, and Jeffreys’ test. The exact binomial test relies on the discrete binomial distribution of the number of defaults, while the z-score and Jeffreys’ tests rely on a continuous approximation of the binomial proportion distribution.
The z-score test is traditionally used in both one-sided and two-sided forms, whereas the exact binomial test is often perceived by practitioners as lacking a natural two-sided extension. While this may be true within the standard hypothesis testing framework, a two-sided p-value for the exact binomial test can nevertheless be calculated.
The purpose of this presentation is to demonstrate how to calculate the p-value of the two-sided exact binomial test. It first introduces the basics of the exact binomial test and its application in the backtesting of PD models, and then provides R and Python examples for calculating the p-value.
As with all tests used for backtesting PD models (including those that assume correlated defaults), the results rely on specific assumptions and align to varying degrees with the IRB framework. Practitioners are therefore encouraged to consider these aspects when using and interpreting the results.
