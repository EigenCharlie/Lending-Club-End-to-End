In IRB modeling, risk quantification is a regulatory requirement aimed at assigning an appropriate level of risk to the model output. This process includes several steps, from assigning the so-called best or base estimate to ultimately adding a certain level of conservatism. For the most common PD model design, where the final model output is a rating scale, assigning the best estimate refers to the calculation of long-run average default rates over the full calibration sample. As a prerequisite for this process, practitioners must be able to assign a rating grade to all relevant observations in the calibration sample.
By calculating best estimates as long-run averages of default rates per rating grade, practitioners lose the link between the model scores (the initial model output) and the final estimates. Maintaining this link between the initial model output and the calibrated best estimates can be beneficial, as it provides a critical input for further analyses of model structure quality. In particular, it can serve as a key input for homogeneity testing, which in IRB modeling is likely the least quantitatively developed aspect of model validation.
My latest presentation introduced a model-based approach to discrete PD rating scale calibration based on optimizing the intercept in a logistic regression. The presented method is one of the approaches that aligns with the most commonly used approach for calibrating a PD rating scale based on calibration segment default rates, and it was therefore selected as the first method in my work dedicated to this topic.
As this is only one of several possible approaches to model-based calibration, I encourage practitioners to consider the advantages and disadvantages of different methods that can be used for the same purpose.
