An insightful working paper from Danmarks Nationalbank (No. 216, September 2025) authored by Henrik Yde Andersen, Camilla Skovbo Christensen, Claus Thustrup Kreiner, and Søren Leth-Petersen explores an intriguing question: Do individuals who stay inactive in one financial decision - such as not refinancing a mortgage - also tend to be inactive in others, like adjusting retirement savings? Using Danish administrative data and quasi-experimental methods, the study finds a striking independence between financial decisions. In other words, those who remain inactive in one context are not systematically inactive in another. This finding challenges the idea that financial inaction stems from stable personal traits (like low literacy or attention) - instead, it highlights how context-specific factors play a much larger role. The implication: financial inaction - and its costs - are broadly distributed rather than concentrated among certain groups.
