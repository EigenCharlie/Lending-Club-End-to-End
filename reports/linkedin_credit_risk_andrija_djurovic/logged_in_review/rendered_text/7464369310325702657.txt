Most credit risk practitioners use this table almost on a daily basis. But why exactly these thresholds and not some others? Partly because they worked reasonably well in practice. And partly because, over time, they became embedded into the industry’s collective intuition: - passed from practitioner to practitioner, - from validator to bank, - from mentor to analyst. The interesting part is that the mathematical foundations needed to formalize this existed long ago. - Information theory. - Likelihood ratios. - Statistical divergence measures. Credit risk modeling had access to these tools for years. Yet much of the industry continued relying on inherited interpretation rules and rules of thumb. I increasingly think credit risk evolved operationally faster than academically. A surprising amount of knowledge in this field still propagates through institutional memory rather than formal statistical standardization. Recently I came across an interesting paper attempting to statistically formalize IV significance testing. Link to the paper — in the comments.
